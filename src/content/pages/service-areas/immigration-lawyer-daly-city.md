### Immigration Detention and Habeas Corpus Cases

When someone is held in immigration detention, the family usually has two questions: where are they, and how do we get them out. In many cases the answer involves a petition for a writ of habeas corpus, which asks a federal judge to review whether the detention is lawful.

Maisoun focuses on this kind of federal litigation. ### Deportation and Removal Defense

Being placed in removal proceedings does not mean you will be deported. It means the government has started a case, and you have the right to respond to it. A removal defense attorney looks at how you entered, how long you have been here, your family ties, any criminal history, and whether you qualify for relief such as asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, or a waiver.

We appear in immigration court, prepare the evidence, and make sure arguments are on the record in case an appeal becomes necessary.

### Asylum Applications

Asylum is for people who fear persecution because of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. These cases are won or lost on detail: dates, documents, consistent testimony, and country conditions evidence. ## More About Immigration Law

Immigration law is the body of federal law that governs who may enter, live, and work in the United States, and what happens when someone is accused of violating those rules. It is mostly set by the Immigration and Nationality Act and enforced by agencies including USCIS, ICE, and CBP, with cases heard in immigration courts run by the Executive Office for Immigration Review.

Key areas include:

- **Family-based immigration:** Petitions by U.S. citizens and permanent residents for spouses, children, parents, and siblings.
- **Asylum and related protection:** Relief for people who fear persecution or torture in their home country.
- **Removal (deportation) defense:** Defending against charges that someone is removable and seeking relief.
- **Detention and bond:** Rules on when the government may hold someone, and when a bond hearing is required.
- **Appeals and federal review:** Challenges to decisions before the BIA and in federal court, including habeas corpus petitions under which a judge reviews whether detention is lawful.

Immigration law changes frequently through policy memos, executive actions, and court rulings. Advice that was right last year may not be right today, which is a good reason to speak with a current, qualified attorney.
