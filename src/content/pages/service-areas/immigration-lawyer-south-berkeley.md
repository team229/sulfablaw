## Immigration Law Services We Offer in South Berkeley

Our practice is centered on cases where a lot is at stake and the government is on the other side. Here is how that breaks down.

### Deportation and Removal Defense

If you are in removal proceedings, an immigration judge will decide whether you can stay in the United States. A **removal defense attorney** looks at how you entered, how long you have been here, your family ties, any criminal history, and whether you qualify for relief such as asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, or a waiver.

#### What Happens at a First Hearing

The first appearance is usually a master calendar hearing. The judge confirms your identity, asks whether you admit or deny the charges, and sets a schedule. Going to this hearing without a lawyer, or skipping it, can lead to a removal order in your absence. If you have a hearing date and no attorney yet, call us before that date.

### Asylum Applications and Representation

An **asylum lawyer** helps you show that you fear persecution because of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. Asylum cases depend on detailed, consistent testimony and solid supporting evidence: country condition reports, medical or psychological evaluations, witness statements, and documents. There is also a one-year filing deadline with limited exceptions, so timing matters.

### Immigration Detention and Bond Hearings

When someone is detained, the first question is whether they are eligible for a bond hearing and how quickly one can be requested. Our **immigration detention lawyer** work includes requesting bond, preparing evidence of community ties and lack of danger or flight risk, and, where the government refuses to provide a hearing it should provide, going to federal court.

### Federal Immigration Litigation and Habeas Corpus

A habeas corpus petition asks a federal judge to review whether detention is lawful. This is a core part of Maisoun's practice. It is the tool used when someone is held without a hearing, re-detained without notice, or kept in custody longer than the law allows.

As an **immigration habeas corpus attorney**, she files petitions and, when the situation is urgent, emergency motions for a temporary restraining order. Federal court is a different environment from immigration court, with its own rules, deadlines, and judges, and the filings have to be precise.

### Family-Based Immigration Petitions

Family petitions look simple on paper and fail over small details: an incomplete affidavit of support, mismatched names across documents, an unresolved prior overstay. A **family-based immigration attorney** reviews eligibility before you file, prepares the petition, and handles requests for evidence from USCIS. We also advise on green card applications, visa options, and related USCIS matters.

### Immigration Appeals

If an immigration judge rules against you, the deadline to appeal is short, usually 30 days. An **immigration appeals lawyer** evaluates whether there were legal or procedural errors and whether an appeal makes sense for your situation.

## Federal Court Results: What the Public Record Shows

We are careful about how we talk about outcomes. Every case turns on its own facts, and past results do not guarantee a similar result in yours. That said, the public court record shows the kind of work Maisoun does in federal court. In 2026 alone:

- **Correa-Correa v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: She filed a habeas petition and an emergency motion. The court granted a preliminary injunction, ordered the petitioner released immediately, and restricted re-detention without notice and a pre-deprivation hearing before a neutral adjudicator, subject to exigent circumstances.
- **Nganda-Mekumba v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: After initial release and re-detention restrictions, the court granted the habeas petition on June 4, 2026, and ordered that the petitioner not be re-detained without constitutional protections.
- **Charaf v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court granted the petition and ordered the petitioner released, subject to the conditions of his existing parole.
- **Bertis Bermudez v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: The court granted emergency relief, ordering release under the prior conditions and restricting further detention without notice and a pre-detention hearing.
- **Lopez-Aldama v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court ordered an individualized bond hearing within seven days or release under tailored conditions. Other requested relief was denied, which is a useful reminder that outcomes are often partial.

These cases show a pattern worth understanding: when detention happens without process, federal courts are sometimes willing to step in. Whether that applies to your family member depends on their history, their current status, and the facts of the arrest.

## What to Do Right Now If a Loved One Was Detained

The first 48 hours matter. Here is a practical checklist.

### Gather the Basics

- Full legal name, date of birth, and country of birth
- The A-number (Alien Registration Number), if you have it
- Where and when the arrest happened
- Any paperwork the person was given, including a Notice to Appear or a bond notice
- Prior immigration history: how and when they entered, any previous applications, any prior court dates

### Locate the Person

The ICE Online Detainee Locator can show where someone is being held. People are often moved quickly, sometimes outside the Bay Area.

### Do Not Sign Anything Without Advice

Detained people are sometimes handed forms, including voluntary departure paperwork, and told to sign. Signing can end your options. Ask to speak to a lawyer first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What does an immigration lawyer do?

An immigration lawyer advises on and represents people in matters such as removal defense, asylum, family petitions, green cards, visas, and detention. They prepare filings, gather evidence, represent clients before USCIS, immigration courts, and federal courts, and explain the risks of each option.

#### Can an immigration attorney get someone out of detention?

Sometimes. Depending on the person's history and the circumstances of the arrest, options may include a bond hearing before an immigration judge or a habeas corpus petition in federal court. Eligibility varies, and there is no guarantee. A prompt review is the best way to find out what applies.

#### What is a habeas corpus petition in immigration cases?

It is a federal court filing that challenges the legality of someone's detention. Courts have used it to order bond hearings, require notice before re-detention, and in some cases order release.

#### Do I need a lawyer for a family-based green card?

You are not legally required to have one, but mistakes can cause delays, denials, or serious problems if you have a prior overstay, unlawful presence, or criminal history. A short consultation before filing often prevents expensive errors.

#### How long does an asylum case take?

It depends on whether the case is filed with USCIS or in immigration court, and on current backlogs. Some cases take months and others take years. An attorney can give a more realistic estimate after reviewing where your case stands.

## About Immigration Law: What You Should Know

Immigration law covers the rules that decide who may enter, live, work, and remain in the United States. It is federal law, mainly the Immigration and Nationality Act, along with regulations, agency policy, and court decisions. Because enforcement priorities and agency policies change, the same set of facts can lead to different outcomes depending on timing.

The main areas include family-based immigration, employment and visa categories, asylum and humanitarian protection, removal defense, and challenges to detention. Cases can move through USCIS, immigration court, the Board of Immigration Appeals, and federal court. Each forum has its own deadlines, and missing one can close off options.

If you are unsure which category your case falls into, a consultation with an immigration lawyer is the fastest way to find out.
