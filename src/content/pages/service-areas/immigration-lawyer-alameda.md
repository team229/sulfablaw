## Immigration Law Firm Services in Alameda

### Immigration Detention and Habeas Corpus Petitions

Federal habeas corpus is a central part of Maisoun's practice. A habeas petition asks a federal judge to decide whether the government has the legal authority to keep someone detained. These cases are fast-moving. Often there is an emergency motion for a temporary restraining order, because the person may be moved or held while the case is pending.

Typical issues include:

- Detention without an individualized bond hearing.
- Re-detention of someone who had been released, without notice or a hearing.
- Claims that continued detention violates due process rights.

If you are searching for an immigration detention lawyer or bond hearing attorney in Alameda, call right away. Timing can decide which options exist.

### Deportation and Removal Defense

A removal case starts when the government files charges in immigration court. It does not end in deportation automatically. As a removal defense attorney, Maisoun reviews your entry, history, family ties, and any criminal record to identify relief. Depending on the facts, that could be asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, a waiver, or another form of protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What does an immigration attorney do?

An immigration attorney advises on visas, green cards, family petitions, asylum, citizenship, removal defense, and detention. The work includes preparing applications, gathering evidence, appearing before USCIS and immigration judges, and filing in federal court when necessary.

#### What is a habeas corpus petition in an immigration case?

It is a federal court filing that asks a judge to review whether a person's detention is lawful. In immigration cases, petitions often argue that someone was held without a bond hearing or re-detained without due process.

#### How much does an immigration lawyer cost?

Fees depend on the type of case and how much work it requires. A straightforward application costs much less than a removal defense case or federal litigation. We explain the cost at your consultation.

#### Can a lawyer help with a denied application?

Often, yes. Depending on the reason for denial and the deadlines, options may include a motion to reopen or reconsider, an appeal, a new filing, or federal court review. Don't assume a denial is the end.

#### Do I need a lawyer for a green card?

Not always, but it is wise to have one if you've had a visa overstay, an arrest, a prior denial, or any uncertainty about eligibility. An avoidable mistake can lead to delays or put you in removal proceedings.

#### Where will my immigration court hearing be?

For most Bay Area cases, hearings are in San Francisco. Your notice lists the exact location and time. If you move, update your address with the court right away.

#### Do you meet with clients in Alameda?

We serve Alameda and the East Bay, with consultations by phone, video, or appointment in person.

#### Can you help if my family member is detained outside the Bay Area?

Yes. People detained by ICE are often held in distant facilities. Because we are admitted in all four California federal districts, we can file habeas petitions where the person is held.

#### What should I bring to a consultation?

Bring notices from ICE, USCIS, or the court, IDs and passports, prior applications, and a timeline of your immigration history. For a detained person, bring their full name and A-number if you have it.

## About Immigration Law

Immigration law is the federal body of law that controls who may enter, remain, and work in the United States, and what happens when someone is accused of violating those rules. It is mostly found in the Immigration and Nationality Act and is carried out by agencies including USCIS, ICE, and CBP. Cases are heard in immigration courts run by the Executive Office for Immigration Review, with appeals to the Board of Immigration Appeals and review in federal court.

Major areas include:

- **Family-based immigration:** Petitions by U.S. citizens and permanent residents for relatives.
- **Asylum and related protection:** Relief for people who fear persecution or torture.
- **Removal defense:** Contesting charges of deportability and applying for relief.
- **Detention and bond:** Rules about when and how long someone may be held, and when a bond hearing is required.
- **Appeals and federal litigation:** Review of agency and court decisions, including habeas corpus.

Immigration policy can change quickly through memos, executive orders, and court decisions. What was true last year may not apply today, so it helps to work with an attorney who follows current developments.
