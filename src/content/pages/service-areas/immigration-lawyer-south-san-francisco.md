## Immigration Law Firm Services for South San Francisco Clients

### Immigration Detention and Habeas Corpus

When ICE detains someone, families need answers fast: Where are they? Can they get out? What are the rights involved?

One tool is a federal habeas corpus petition. It asks a judge to decide whether the detention is legal. Maisoun handles these cases and files emergency motions for temporary restraining orders when a person is at risk of being moved or when they were re-detained without notice. Common arguments involve due process rights, including a hearing before a neutral decision-maker and a meaningful chance to request bond.

If you need an immigration detention lawyer or bond hearing attorney near South San Francisco, call immediately and have the person's full name and A-number ready if you can.

### Deportation and Removal Defense

Being placed in removal proceedings does not mean you will be deported. It means you have a case to defend. As a removal defense attorney, Maisoun evaluates how you entered the U.S., your length of residence, your family, and your record, and then determines whether you qualify for asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, a waiver, or another form of relief.

### Asylum Applications

Asylum protects people who fear persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. A strong application depends on a consistent timeline, supporting documents, and reliable country-conditions evidence. We help clients prepare each of those, and we get them ready for interviews and hearings.

## About Immigration Law

Immigration law is the set of federal rules that decide who may enter, live, and work in the United States, and what happens when someone is accused of violating those rules. The main source is the Immigration and Nationality Act. Agencies such as USCIS, ICE, and CBP administer and enforce it, immigration courts run by the Executive Office for Immigration Review hear cases, and decisions can be appealed to the Board of Immigration Appeals and reviewed in federal court.

Core areas include:

- **Family-based immigration:** Petitions by U.S. citizens and permanent residents for relatives.
- **Employment-based and work visas:** Status for professionals and workers sponsored by employers.
- **Asylum and related protection:** Relief for people fleeing persecution or torture.
- **Removal defense:** Contesting deportation charges and seeking relief.
- **Detention and bond:** Limits on how and when the government may hold someone, and when a bond hearing is required.
- **Appeals and federal litigation:** Review of agency and court decisions, including habeas corpus petitions.

The rules change often through policy memos, executive actions, and court decisions. Because advice that was correct last year may be outdated now, it is worth consulting an attorney who tracks current developments.
