## Immigration Law Services We Offer in San Bruno

### Deportation and Removal Defense

If you are in removal proceedings, an immigration judge will decide whether you can remain in the United States. A **removal defense attorney** reviews how you entered, how long you have lived here, your family ties, any criminal history, and whether you qualify for relief such as asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, or a waiver.

#### Why the First Hearing Matters

The first appearance is typically a master calendar hearing. The judge asks whether you admit or deny the charges and sets a schedule. Missing it can result in an order of removal in your absence. If you have a hearing date and no lawyer, call before that date, not after.

### Asylum Applications and Representation

An **asylum lawyer** helps you show that you fear persecution on account of race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. These cases are built on consistent testimony and strong evidence: country condition reports, medical or psychological evaluations, witness statements, and records. There is also a one-year filing deadline with limited exceptions, so waiting can hurt your case.

### Immigration Detention and Bond Hearings

When someone is detained, the first questions are whether they are eligible for a bond hearing and how quickly one can be requested. Our **immigration detention lawyer** and **bond hearing attorney** work includes preparing evidence of family ties, community support, and lack of danger or flight risk. When the government will not provide a hearing the law requires, the next step may be federal court.

### Federal Immigration Litigation and Habeas Corpus

A habeas corpus petition asks a federal judge to decide whether detention is lawful. It is a major part of Maisoun's practice. People use it when they are held without a hearing, re-detained without notice, or kept in custody in ways that raise due process concerns.

As an **immigration habeas corpus attorney** and **federal immigration lawyer**, she files petitions and, in urgent situations, emergency motions for temporary restraining orders. Federal court has its own rules and deadlines, and the first filing often sets the tone for the whole case.

### Family-Based Immigration and Green Cards

Family petitions look simple on paper and run into trouble over small things: an incomplete affidavit of support, names that do not match across documents, an old overstay nobody mentioned. A **family-based immigration attorney** and **green card lawyer** checks eligibility before you file, prepares the petition, and responds to USCIS requests for evidence. We also advise on visas and other USCIS matters.

### Immigration Appeals

If an immigration judge rules against you, the deadline to appeal is short, usually 30 days. An **immigration appeals lawyer** looks for legal or procedural errors and tells you honestly whether an appeal makes sense.

## Federal Court Results: What the Public Record Shows

Every case turns on its own facts, and past results do not guarantee a similar outcome. Still, the public docket shows the kind of federal work Maisoun does. In 2026:

- **Correa-Correa v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: Habeas petition and emergency motion. The court granted a preliminary injunction, ordered immediate release, and restricted re-detention without notice and a pre-deprivation hearing before a neutral adjudicator, subject to exigent circumstances.
- **Nganda-Mekumba v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: After initial release, the court granted the habeas petition on June 4, 2026, and ordered that the petitioner not be re-detained without constitutional protections.
- **Charaf v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court granted the petition and ordered release, subject to the conditions of the petitioner's existing parole.
- **Bertis Bermudez v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: Emergency relief granted, with release under prior conditions and limits on further detention without notice and a pre-detention hearing.
- **Lopez-Aldama v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court ordered an individualized bond hearing within seven days or release under tailored conditions. Other requested relief was denied, a reminder that outcomes can be partial.

The pattern is worth understanding. When detention happens without adequate process, federal courts are sometimes willing to step in. Whether that applies to your family member depends on their history, status, and the circumstances of the arrest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Service-Related Questions

#### What does an immigration lawyer do?

An immigration lawyer advises and represents people in matters such as removal defense, asylum, family petitions, green cards, visas, and detention. They prepare filings, gather evidence, appear before USCIS, immigration courts, and federal courts, and explain the risks of each option.

#### Can an immigration attorney help someone get out of detention?

Sometimes. Depending on the person's history and the facts of the arrest, options may include a bond hearing before an immigration judge or a habeas corpus petition in federal court. Eligibility varies and nothing is guaranteed, so a prompt review is the best way to learn what applies.

#### What is a habeas corpus petition in an immigration case?

It is a federal court filing that challenges the legality of someone's detention. Courts have used it to order bond hearings, require notice before re-detention, and in some cases order release.

#### Do I need a lawyer for a family-based green card?

No law requires it, but mistakes can cause delays, denials, or bigger problems if there is a prior overstay, unlawful presence, or criminal history. A short consultation before filing often prevents costly errors.

#### How long does an asylum case take?

It depends on whether the case is with USCIS or in immigration court, and on current backlogs. Some cases take months and others take years. After reviewing where your case stands, an attorney can give a more realistic estimate.

## About Immigration Law: What You Should Know

Immigration law governs who may enter, live, work, and remain in the United States. It is federal law, built on the Immigration and Nationality Act, agency regulations and policy, and court decisions. Because enforcement priorities and policies change, the same facts can lead to different results depending on timing.

The main areas include family-based immigration, employment and visa categories, asylum and humanitarian protection, removal defense, and challenges to unlawful detention. Cases can move through USCIS, immigration court, the Board of Immigration Appeals, and federal court, and each forum has its own deadlines. Missing one can close off options.

If you are not sure which category your case falls into, a consultation with an immigration lawyer is the fastest way to find out.
