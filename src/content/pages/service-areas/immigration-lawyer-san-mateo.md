## Immigration Law Services We Offer in San Mateo

### Deportation and Removal Defense

If you are in removal proceedings, an immigration judge decides whether you can stay. A **deportation defense lawyer** or **removal defense attorney** reviews your entry, time in the country, family ties, criminal history if any, and whether you may qualify for relief such as asylum, cancellation of removal, adjustment of status, or a waiver.

#### What Happens at the First Hearing

The first appearance is usually a master calendar hearing. The judge asks whether you admit or deny the allegations and sets next steps. Missing the hearing can lead to a removal order issued in your absence. If you already have a date, call us before it arrives.

### Asylum Applications and Representation

An **asylum lawyer** helps you show a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group. These cases rest on credible, consistent testimony and solid evidence such as country reports, expert or medical evaluations, and witness statements. A one-year filing deadline applies, with limited exceptions.

### Immigration Detention and Bond Hearings

When someone is detained, the immediate questions are whether they are eligible for a bond hearing and how fast it can be scheduled. As an **immigration detention lawyer** and **bond hearing attorney**, we prepare evidence of family ties, community support, and lack of danger or flight risk. If the government does not provide a hearing the law requires, federal court may be the next step.

### Federal Immigration Litigation and Habeas Corpus

A habeas corpus petition asks a federal judge to review whether detention is lawful. It is a core part of Maisoun's practice. It comes into play when someone is held without a hearing, re-detained without notice, or kept in custody in a way that raises due process concerns.

As an **immigration habeas corpus attorney** and **federal immigration lawyer**, she files petitions and, when time is short, emergency motions for temporary restraining orders. Federal court has its own rules and deadlines, and the first filing matters.

### Family-Based Immigration, Green Cards, and Visas

Family petitions often fail on small details: an incomplete affidavit of support, mismatched names across documents, or an old overstay no one mentioned. A **family-based immigration attorney** and **green card lawyer** checks eligibility before filing, prepares the petition, and responds to USCIS requests for evidence. We also advise on visa options and other USCIS matters.

### Immigration Appeals

If an immigration judge rules against you, the deadline to appeal is short, usually 30 days. An **immigration appeals lawyer** reviews the record for legal or procedural errors and tells you plainly whether an appeal is worth pursuing.

## Federal Court Results: What the Public Record Shows

Every case depends on its own facts, and past results do not guarantee a similar outcome. The public docket does show the kind of federal work Maisoun handles. In 2026:

- **Correa-Correa v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: Habeas petition with an emergency motion. The court granted a preliminary injunction, ordered immediate release, and restricted re-detention without notice and a pre-deprivation hearing before a neutral adjudicator, subject to exigent circumstances.
- **Nganda-Mekumba v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: After an initial release order, the court granted the habeas petition on June 4, 2026, and ordered that the petitioner not be re-detained without constitutional protections.
- **Charaf v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court granted the petition and ordered release, subject to the conditions of the petitioner's existing parole.
- **Bertis Bermudez v. Albarran (E.D. Cal.)**: Emergency relief was granted, with release under prior conditions and limits on further detention without notice and a pre-detention hearing.
- **Lopez-Aldama v. Rios (C.D. Cal.)**: The court ordered an individualized bond hearing within seven days or release under tailored conditions. Other requested relief was denied, a reminder that outcomes can be partial.

The takeaway: when detention happens without adequate process, federal courts are sometimes willing to intervene. Whether that applies to your situation depends on your history, current status, and the facts of the arrest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What does an immigration lawyer do?

An immigration lawyer advises and represents people in matters such as removal defense, asylum, family petitions, green cards, visas, and detention. They prepare filings, gather evidence, appear before USCIS, immigration courts, and federal courts, and explain the risks of each option.

#### Can an immigration attorney help someone get out of detention?

Sometimes. Depending on the person's history and the facts of the arrest, options may include a bond hearing before an immigration judge or a habeas corpus petition in federal court. Eligibility varies and nothing is guaranteed, so a prompt review is the best way to learn what applies.

#### What is a habeas corpus petition in an immigration case?

It is a federal court filing that challenges the legality of someone's detention. Courts have used it to order bond hearings, require notice before re-detention, and in some cases order release.

#### Do I need a lawyer for a family-based green card?

No law requires it, but mistakes can cause delays, denials, or bigger problems if there is a prior overstay, unlawful presence, or criminal history. A short consultation before filing often prevents costly errors.

#### How long does an asylum case take?

It depends on whether the case is with USCIS or in immigration court, and on current backlogs. Some cases take months and others take years. After reviewing where your case stands, an attorney can give a more realistic estimate.

## About Immigration Law: What You Should Know

Immigration law governs who may enter, live, work, and remain in the United States. It is federal law, built on the Immigration and Nationality Act, agency regulations and policy, and court decisions. Because enforcement priorities and policies change, the same facts can lead to different results depending on timing.

The main areas include family-based immigration, employment and visa categories, asylum and humanitarian protection, removal defense, and challenges to unlawful detention. Cases can move through USCIS, immigration court, the Board of Immigration Appeals, and federal court, and each forum has its own deadlines. Missing one can close off options.

If you are not sure which category your case falls into, a consultation with an immigration lawyer is the fastest way to find out.
