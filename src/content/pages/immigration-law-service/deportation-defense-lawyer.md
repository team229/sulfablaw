## What Deportation Defense Actually Means

People use "deportation" and "removal" interchangeably. Legally, the government's process is called removal proceedings, and it takes place in immigration court. A removal defense attorney's job is to challenge the government's case, find any form of relief you may qualify for, and present both to the judge.

There are two broad tracks to every case:

- **Fighting the charge.** The government has to prove it has grounds to remove you. Sometimes the charge is wrong, the paperwork is defective, or the legal theory doesn't hold up.
- **Seeking relief.** Even if the charge is valid, the law offers several forms of protection or status that allow people to stay.

A good defense usually looks at both from day one.

## Why Immigration Judges Find People Removable

Understanding the government's reasoning is the first step in answering it. These are among the most common grounds.

### Entering or Staying Without Proper Authorization

This includes entering without inspection, using a visa for a purpose it wasn't issued for, or staying beyond the date on your I-94 record. Many people in this group are surprised to learn they have options, such as adjustment of status through a family member, depending on how they entered and what has happened since.

### Criminal Convictions

Some convictions carry serious immigration consequences, including certain drug offenses, domestic violence offenses, crimes involving moral turpitude, and offenses classified as aggravated felonies. The label a state court uses isn't always the label immigration law applies, which is why the exact statute, sentence, and record of conviction all matter. If you have a criminal case pending, talk to an immigration attorney before you accept a plea.

### Misrepresentation or Fraud

A false statement on an application, or a claim to U.S. citizenship, can lead to removal charges and long bars to returning. These cases are fact-sensitive, and sometimes the original statement wasn't what the government says it was.

### Missed Court Dates and Old Orders

If you missed a hearing, a judge may have ordered you removed in your absence. That order isn't always final. A motion to reopen can be filed in certain situations, such as when notice wasn't properly given or when exceptional circumstances kept you from attending.

## How Removal Proceedings Work in San Francisco Immigration Court

The steps are fairly consistent, though timelines vary widely.

### The Notice to Appear

The government starts the case by serving a Notice to Appear (NTA), which lists the charges and alleges facts about your immigration history. We review it for errors, including wrong dates, wrong charges, and missing information. Not every NTA error ends a case, but some give us useful arguments.

### The Master Calendar Hearing

This is a short, preliminary hearing. The judge confirms your identity, asks whether you admit or deny the allegations, and sets deadlines. How you plead matters, and it's a decision to make with an attorney, not at the counter. If you don't have a lawyer yet, you can usually ask for time to find one.

### Filing Applications for Relief

If you're eligible for relief, we prepare and file the application, along with evidence such as identity documents, country conditions reports, medical or psychological records, tax returns, and letters from family and community members.

### The Individual Hearing

This is the trial portion. You testify, witnesses may testify, and the government's attorney may cross-examine. The judge then issues a decision, sometimes the same day and sometimes in writing later. Preparation here is everything: consistent testimony, organized evidence, and a legal theory that fits the facts.

### Appeals

If the judge rules against you, the next step is generally an appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals, and the deadline is short, typically 30 days. After that, some cases can be taken to the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals. Missing an appeal deadline can end your options, so don't wait.

## Forms of Relief a Removal Defense Lawyer Can Pursue

Which relief fits depends on your history, your family, and the conditions you'd face if sent back.

- **Asylum, withholding of removal, and Convention Against Torture protection.** For people who fear persecution or torture. Asylum has a one-year filing deadline with exceptions, so timing matters.
- **Cancellation of removal.** Available in different forms to certain lawful permanent residents and to certain long-term residents who can show hardship to qualifying U.S. citizen or permanent resident relatives.
- **Adjustment of status.** Applying for a green card through a qualifying family member or other basis, sometimes inside removal proceedings.
- **Waivers.** Forgiveness of certain violations or inadmissibility grounds, often tied to a qualifying relative.
- **U and T visas.** For victims of certain crimes or human trafficking who cooperated with law enforcement.
- **Prosecutorial discretion and administrative closure.** Depending on current policy, the government may agree to pause or close a case. This changes with enforcement priorities, so we check the current rules.
- **Voluntary departure.** A narrower option that can avoid a formal removal order and its long-term penalties, though it requires careful weighing.

A candid review of your history sometimes uncovers relief you didn't know about. It also sometimes shows that a tempting option won't work, and it's better to learn that early.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Service-Related Questions

#### 1. What should I do first if I receive a Notice to Appear?

Don't ignore it, and don't sign anything you don't understand. Note the hearing date if one is listed, gather your documents, and speak with a deportation attorney as soon as possible. Missing a hearing can lead to an order of removal in your absence.

#### 2. Can I be deported if I have a green card?

Yes, in some cases. Certain criminal convictions, fraud findings, or abandonment of residence can lead to removal charges against permanent residents. But a green card also gives access to forms of relief that others don't have, so speak with an attorney before assuming the worst.

#### 3. How long do removal proceedings take?

It varies widely. Detained cases can resolve in weeks or a few months. Non-detained cases can take much longer, sometimes years, depending on the court's schedule and the complexity of your case. An attorney can give you a realistic estimate after reviewing your file.

#### 4. Do I need a lawyer in immigration court?

You have the right to be represented, but the government doesn't provide a lawyer for you in immigration court. Removal cases involve complicated law and procedure, and having an immigration court attorney usually makes a big difference in how well your case is presented.

#### 5. What if the judge orders me removed?

A removal order isn't always the end. You may be able to appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals, ask for review in the Ninth Circuit in some cases, or file a motion to reopen. Deadlines are tight, so contact an attorney immediately.

### Location-Related Questions
