## Immigration Attorney for Deportation and Asylum

Deportation defense and asylum often overlap, which is why people look for one immigration attorney who handles both.

### Deportation and Removal Defense

Removal proceedings begin with a Notice to Appear. From there, an immigration judge decides whether you may stay. A removal defense attorney reviews the charges, checks the notice for errors and looks for every form of relief you may qualify for.

#### Common Defenses and Relief

- Asylum, withholding of removal and protection under the Convention Against Torture
- Cancellation of removal for certain long-term residents
- Adjustment of status through a family petition
- Challenges to the government's evidence or the legality of the charge
- Motions to reopen or appeals when an earlier decision was wrong

#### What We Look at First

We start with your immigration history, your family ties and any prior orders or applications. A small detail, like how you entered or an old filing, can change which defenses are open.

### Asylum Applications and Hearings

Asylum protects people who fear persecution because of race, religion, nationality, political opinion or membership in a particular social group. The law is demanding and the evidence matters as much as the story.

#### How We Prepare an Asylum Case

We help you build a clear, consistent declaration, collect country-condition reports and supporting witnesses, and prepare you for the interview or hearing. Inconsistencies are one of the most common reasons claims fail, so we spend real time on dates, places and details before anything is filed.

#### The One-Year Deadline

Asylum generally must be filed within one year of arrival, with limited exceptions. If that date is close or has passed, speak with an asylum attorney quickly.

## Immigration Detention and Federal Litigation Lawyer

This is the part of immigration law where Maisoun's practice stands out. When someone is detained, waiting for the ordinary process can mean weeks or months in custody. Federal court can move faster.

### Immigration Detention and Bond Hearings

If a loved one is detained, we first find out where they are held, why, and whether they are eligible for a bond hearing. A bond hearing before an immigration judge is often the first real opportunity to argue for release.

### Habeas Corpus Petitions

A habeas corpus petition asks a federal judge to decide whether detention is lawful. We file these petitions together with emergency motions for a temporary restraining order when someone has just been taken into custody or re-detained without notice.

#### Due Process in Detention Cases

Our petitions focus on due process rights: notice, a hearing before a neutral decision-maker and a lawful basis for holding someone. In recent cases in the Eastern and Central Districts of California, federal courts ordered release, ordered bond hearings and restricted re-detention without proper process. ## Family-Based Petitions, Green Cards and USCIS Matters

Not every case is an emergency. Many people come to us simply wanting to file correctly.

### Family-Based Immigration

U.S. citizens and permanent residents can petition for certain relatives. The right path depends on the relationship, the petitioner's status and whether the relative is inside or outside the United States. Immediate relatives of U.S. citizens generally avoid the long visa backlogs that affect other categories.

### Green Card and Adjustment of Status

Adjustment of status lets eligible people apply for a green card without leaving the country. It is paperwork-heavy and unforgiving of gaps. We check eligibility, prior entries and any past issues before filing, because a denied application can lead to bigger problems.

### USCIS Representation

A USCIS lawyer can respond to requests for evidence, prepare you for interviews and address delays or denials. Immigration policy changes often, so we confirm current requirements before every filing instead of relying on what worked last year.

## Immigration Legal Services in California: Which Forum Handles Your Case

Immigration law is federal, but in California the practical details depend on which body is deciding your case. Choosing the wrong path wastes time you may not have.

| **Forum** | **What it decides** | **When it applies** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immigration court | Removal cases, asylum defenses, bond hearings | You have a Notice to Appear or are detained and in proceedings |
| USCIS | Green cards, family petitions, many applications | You are applying for a benefit and are not in removal proceedings |
| Federal district court | Lawfulness of detention, unlawful agency action | Detention is unlawful or the process is denied or delayed |

These paths can overlap. A person in removal proceedings may also have a pending family petition, and a detained client may need both a bond request and a habeas petition. Part of our job is sequencing them so one filing helps rather than harms the other.

If you are searching for immigration legal services in California, the most useful first step is a case review. Bring any notices, receipts, court papers and a timeline of your entry and filings. Even incomplete records help.

## About Immigration Law Services in California

Immigration law is federal, yet outcomes depend on the forum. Immigration judges decide removal cases, USCIS handles most applications and petitions, and federal courts review whether detention is lawful and whether agencies acted within the law.

People in removal proceedings may qualify for asylum, withholding of removal, Convention Against Torture protection, cancellation of removal or adjustment of status, depending on their facts. People held in immigration detention have due process rights, and a federal court can review the lawfulness of that detention. Related problems include missed deadlines, prior removal orders, re-detention after release, denied bond hearings and long USCIS delays.
